## What these four documents teach the build

**The laminated lists ARE her retail prices — confirmed by Kevin at intake**
("a list of items she sells at what price she sells them at, generally").
Cross-checking them against the Kennicott costs on the same varieties shows
the real markup:

| Variety | Kennicott cost | Laminated list | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leather Leaf | $4.00/bunch | $15.00/bunch | 3.75x |
| Eucalyptus Parvifolia | $10.95/bunch | $33.00/bunch | 3.0x |
| Pittosporum Variegated | $0.95/stem | $3.00/stem | 3.2x |
| Carnation | $0.45/stem | $2.00/stem | 4.4x |
| Ruscus Israeli | $11.95/bunch? | $18.00/bunch | 1.5x |

That is her real markup sitting at roughly x3, which CORROBORATES the
provisional x3 in `quote-math.ts` for the first time with her own paper.
The individual transcribed numbers still get her once-over before they seed
anything live, and ask who Lacey is.

**Two inventory worlds, confirmed.** Plants are par-stocked durables: count,
reorder to par, stable retail and cost on one sheet. Cut flowers are a weekly
perishable prebook whose prices float (hydrangea varieties ranged $1.50 to
$3.60/stem in ONE week). The inventory core needs both shapes, and the plant
side is nearly trivial: the par sheet above is already the schema (item, par,
have, need, cost, retail).

**Bunches vs stems is the modeling gap.** Kennicott sells some varieties by
bunch and some by stem, the Stems/Bunch column arrives blank, and recipes
consume stems. `StemEvent` today only speaks stems. The purchase model needs
unit (bunch|stem) plus a stems-per-bunch factor per variety, owner-supplied
once and reused, or cost-per-stem cannot be computed from a bunch line.

**Distributor vocabulary vs shop vocabulary.** Kennicott says "Alstroemeria
Light Pink Super Select"; her recipes and her laminated list say
"alstroemeria". Ingesting order sheets needs a mapping layer from distributor
line names to `normalizeVariety()` names, learned once per new line item.

**The weekly interaction to digitize is the pen.** The standing order mostly
repeats; the work is the small weekly delta (crossing out White, writing "blk
org yellow"; PALE YELLOW on the carnations). A weekly-order screen should
start from last week's order and make the delta a ten-second edit, then total
it live against the truck-date budget.

**Kennicott facts worth keeping:** Grand Rapids branch, Tuesday standing
order, sales rep Chris Douglass, account D0830, ~$600/week at midsummer, tax
$0.00 (resale). Prebooks are printed confirmations, so a "type in this week's
prebook" screen has a paper source to copy from every single week, which
makes adoption realistic before any Kennicott-side integration exists.
